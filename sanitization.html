Sanitization Core Module

Sanitization & Hygiene Practices

Master the fundamental protocols of hand hygiene, water purification, food safety, environmental cleanliness, and systematic waste disposal.

Hand Hygiene Protocol

The 6 Steps of Proper Handwashing

Follow these 6 steps for at least 20 seconds to eliminate 99.9% of harmful bacteria and viruses from your hands.

1

Wet Hands

Wet your hands thoroughly with clean, running water (warm or cold).

2

Apply Soap

Apply enough liquid, bar, or foam soap to cover all hand surfaces completely.

3

Rub Palms Together

Rub palm to palm to generate rich foam and lather across both hands.

4

Interlace & Scrub

Scrub between fingers, backs of hands, thumbs, and fingertips under nails for 20 seconds.

5

Rinse thoroughly

Rinse all residual soap and loosened germs away with clean running water.

6

Dry Completely

Dry hands thoroughly with a clean single-use towel or fresh air dryer.

Safe Drinking Water Protocols

Unsafe drinking water is a chief vector for enteric diseases such as cholera, dysentery, and hepatitis A.

  • Rolling Boil: Boil water for at least 1 full minute to kill viruses, bacteria, and cysts.
  • Filtration: Use activated carbon or reverse osmosis (RO) filters for sediment and heavy metal removal.
  • Clean Storage: Store treated water in covered, narrow-mouthed vessels to avoid hand contact.

Food Hygiene & Safety

Prevent foodborne illnesses and food poisoning by practicing the 4 WHO core food safety principles.

  • Clean: Wash hands, counters, and utensils thoroughly before and after preparing food.
  • Separate: Keep raw meats separate from cooked items to prevent cross-contamination.
  • Cook: Cook foods to proper safe internal temperatures to destroy bacterial toxins.
Environmental Cleanliness

Waste Segregation at Source

Properly separating household waste prevents landfill overflow, facilitates recycling, and stops disease vectors.

Green Bin

Wet / Organic Waste

Biodegradable materials suitable for community or home composting.

  • Vegetable peels & fruit scraps
  • Leftover cooked food
  • Tea leaves & coffee grounds
Blue Bin

Dry / Recyclable Waste

Non-biodegradable clean materials intended for processing and reuse.

  • Plastic bottles & containers
  • Cardboard, newspapers & paper
  • Glass jars & clean aluminum cans
Red / Black Bin

Hazardous / E-Waste

Toxic, medical, or electronic waste requiring specialist disposal.

  • Expired medicines & bandages
  • Used batteries & lightbulbs
  • Household disinfectant chemicals
